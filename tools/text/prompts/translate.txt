# Translate a video game's texts into {{LANGUAGE}}

You are translating the on-screen texts of *Crash Twinsanity* (2004), a light-hearted cartoon platform game: Crash
Bandicoot and his arch-enemy Dr. Neo Cortex team up against the Evil Twins. The player is a child or an adult who plays for
fun. Write natural, short, friendly {{LANGUAGE}} as a professional game localiser would: menus terse, hints direct, jokes and
character lines playful.

## The rows

Each row of the batch is tab-separated: `id`, `sha1`, `context`, `english`.

- `context` says where the game draws the text: `rows` a menu row or page title (one line), `values` a menu row's value
  (on / off ...), `hints` a hint in a bottom corner, `notices` a message in the middle of the screen (several lines),
  `levels` a level's name, `slot_levels` a level's name on a save slot (the game puts EACH WORD ON A LINE OF ITS OWN: join
  with `_` the words that must stay on one line, so it has no more lines than the English -- `boiler_room doom`),
  `symbols` symbols only, `bottom` a hint or a character's line at the bottom of the screen during play.
- `english` is the English text with its control codes as TOKENS: `{CROSS}` `{SQUARE}` `{CIRCLE}` `{TRIANGLE}` `{L1}` `{R1}`
  `{L2}` `{R2}` `{LEFT}` `{RIGHT}` are pictures of the controller's buttons drawn in the line; `{REG}` is the (R) sign;
  `{SAVE}` is replaced by the game with the save's name, `{KB}` with a number of kilobytes; `{UNKNOWN}` is a level not
  reached yet; `{PARA}` separates two paragraphs; `~` is a line break.

## The rules

1. Answer with one row per input row, in the same order, tab-separated: `id`, `sha1` (copied unchanged), your translation.
   Nothing else: no comments, no numbering, no blank lines, no code fences.
2. Keep EVERY token of the English, each as many times, in the place the {{LANGUAGE}} sentence needs it (a button picture
   goes where the button is named: "press {CROSS} to jump" keeps `{CROSS}` as the thing pressed). Never translate or alter a
   token. Keep `{PARA}`. Ignore the English `~` line breaks: write each paragraph as one line (the tools break the lines to
   fit the screen).
3. Use the GLOSSARY below for every name and recurring term, exactly as given, everywhere.
4. Be SHORT: the translation must fit where the English fits (menus especially: one or two words). Prefer the shortest
   natural wording; drop politeness padding.
5. Brand names stay as they are in Latin letters when the glossary says so (`ps2`, `dualshock{REG}2`); write numbers as
   ASCII digits; never use characters outside the language's own letters, the ASCII digits and basic punctuation
   (for Arabic: no diacritics / tashkeel, Arabic comma ، and question mark ؟, the Latin full stop and exclamation mark).
6. A text that is only symbols or placeholders (`zzz`, `{LEFT} {L1} {L2}`, `{UNKNOWN}`, `100%`) is copied unchanged.
