# TwinsanityEvolved -- what's new since v0.1.8

Crash Twinsanity as a native game for Windows, macOS, Linux and the PS Vita: the twinsanity-reversed decompilation running
on the NativeEE engine. **No game data is included: you play with your own disc image.**

## Install

1. Download the zip for your system from this release (the table at the end names each file), or the launcher (see
   Play below), which does every step for you.
2. Unzip it anywhere, on any drive (Windows: right-click the zip > *Properties* > tick **Unblock** first, so SmartScreen
   does not warn about the unsigned program).
3. Run `twinsanityevolved` (`twinsanityevolved.exe` on Windows; on macOS, the first time, right-click it > **Open**). The
   first time it asks for your disc image: your own Crash Twinsanity `.iso`, the European (PAL) or the North American disc.

Nothing is installed and nothing else is downloaded. Requirements: a graphics driver with OpenGL 4.1 (any current NVIDIA,
AMD, Intel or Apple GPU); keyboard and mouse or a gamepad (Space = cross, Left Shift = square, right mouse = circle,
E = triangle, W A S D = left stick, mouse = camera, Y = start, Tab = select).

## Play

- **The launcher installs the finished game.** It no longer makes the game from its source on your computer: it
  downloads the game for your system from the newest release, checks it against the release's checksums and puts it in your game folder. Its
  first run is one sheet of four questions -- where your game is, where it should live, where you will play (this
  computer, a PS Vita or another computer), then the download with its size and speed -- with Back on every step and
  nothing to type.
- **It finds your disc for you.** It looks in Downloads, Desktop, Documents, its own folder and any removable drive, and
  recognises the disc by what is on it (see Discs). Drop the file on the window if it is somewhere else. A disc you remove
  from the list stays off it on later searches.
- **One game folder holds everything.** `~/Games/Crash Twinsanity` (`%USERPROFILE%\Games\Crash Twinsanity` on Windows)
  unless you choose another: the game, your settings (`ps2r.ini`), your memory cards (`memcards`) and your mods (`mods`).
  Move the folder and everything moves with it: the game reads the `ps2r.ini` beside it first (on macOS, beside the app).
- **Pages: Games, Mods, Settings, About.** Games shows your discs, each with **Play** and, when a new version is out,
  **Update**; an update never overwrites your settings, saves or mods. Settings is the game's own Options in plain words --
  the same `ps2r.ini` the in-game menu writes, so a change in one is a change in the other. About can remove what the
  launcher keeps and says what it could not remove.
- **Launchers for every desktop.** A portable zip for Windows, a `.dmg` for macOS and, new, an AppImage for Linux.
- Quitting the launcher during a download is safe: nothing half-downloaded is left in your game folder.
- Every release zip also holds the mod tool and the Arabic language pack (see Mods and Languages).

- **Save states:** F5 saves the running game to `savestates/slot0.ps2st` in the game folder and F8 loads it back where it
  was; the game says so on screen. On the PS Vita the folder is `ux0:data/twinsanity/savestates/`.

## Discs

- **The European (PAL) disc** (SLES-52568) plays as before.
- **The North American disc plays** (SLUS-20909 v2.00, the "Action Pack" release): choose it and play. It runs at 60
  frames a second (its console's rate), in English with its own voices and movies, and keeps its own memory card
  (`memcards-SLUS-20909` beside `memcards`), so the two discs never overwrite each other's saves. Its program's own code
  changes are played the European way.
- **Every disc is identified by its content**, compared with the known dumps of the original discs (redump.org): the
  launcher shows `PAL · original disc`, `NTSC-U · original disc`, `PAL · modified disc` (the original program with other
  files, such as a translation patch -- it plays like the original) or `PAL · unrecognised version` (accepted with a
  warning). It reads the whole image once, a few seconds, and remembers the answer. The game checks the size at every
  start; `--verify-disc` checks the whole image.
- The Japanese disc and the first North American release (v1.00) are recognised but **not playable in this version**:
  each needs an address map, which `twinsanityevolved-modtool region-map` makes from the two discs for someone who has
  both.

## Mods

- **Mods packaged as Mod Crates are supported** -- the community's usual shape (a `.zip` or a folder with
  `ModCrateInfo.txt`, `layer0/` and `layer1/`), including large ones of over a gigabyte (about half a minute to add). The
  author's notes are shown under the mod.
- **Also taken as they are shipped:** a patched disc image (only the files that differ from your disc are kept), an
  xdelta, PPF or BPS patch made against the original disc, a folder of files laid out like the disc (or a loose
  `CRASH.BD` + `CRASH.BH`), and one file of the game's archive passed around on its own (such as a `Default.rm2`), placed
  by its name.
- **The launcher's Mods page:** drop a mod on the page (or **Add a mod…**) and it is added to your mods folder; each mod
  has a switch, a line saying what it changes and its region; drag the mods into order (the lower one wins where two
  change the same file); **Remove** deletes one after asking. Adding a mod you already have (a new version) asks first,
  then replaces it and keeps its switch and its place. A change takes effect the next time the game starts.
- **The mod tool** (`twinsanityevolved-modtool`, in every zip) does the same on the command line: `import`, `inspect`
  (what a file is, without adding it) and `verify` (every file of a mod checked byte for byte).
- **A mod is checked against your disc.** A mod records the disc it was made over; the game leaves out a mod made for
  another disc and says so in its log, and the Mods page says "Left out on this disc: made for the North American disc."
  where that applies. A loose archive file made for the other release is refused when you add it.
- While any mod is on, the game saves to its own memory card (`memcards-mods`): your normal saves are never touched.
- In the game, OPTIONS > MODS now lists the built-in mods (level select, speedrun mode); the mods of your mods folder are
  switched on the launcher's Mods page.
- **What cannot apply:** code changes in a mod's modified game program (its data changes -- tables, texts, numbers -- do
  apply; the code changes are listed under the mod); Crate Mod Loader's game-code settings (a crate carrying
  `ModCrateSettings.txt` is refused with the reason; settings a crate's notes ask for are named in a warning); xdelta
  patches made with secondary compression; RAR / 7-Zip archives, CHD / CSO / ECM images, `.cue` sheets, UPS / IPS patches;
  emulator texture packs and cheat codes.

- **Where a mod is switched:** a mod that changes the disc's files is switched on and off in the launcher only and is in
  place when the game starts; the game's own OPTIONS > MODS page keeps the two built-in mods (level select, speedrun mode),
  which work while you play. A mod made for the other region's disc is left out, and the launcher's row says so.
- **On the PS Vita** the switch is the `mods.<id>.enabled` line in `ux0:data/twinsanity/ps2r.ini` (the launcher writes it
  into the PS Vita folder; VitaShell's text editor changes it on the console); the mod's files stay on the card either way,
  and modded saves live on their own memory card (`memcards-mods/`).

## Languages

- **The text and the speech are two settings:** GAME OPTIONS > TEXT LANGUAGE and SPEECH LANGUAGE (and the launcher's
  Settings). Any text with any speech: Portuguese text with Spanish voices, Arabic text with French voices. The text
  languages are recognised by what the disc's text banks contain, so on a translated disc "english" is still English. The
  movies' sound and the title cards follow the speech. An older `game.language` setting keeps working.
- **Arabic:** every menu, notice and hint (305 texts, our own translation), drawn right to left in Arabic letters made in
  the game's lettering style. It comes with every zip and the launcher places it in your mods folder; there is nothing to
  switch on: TEXT LANGUAGE offers **arabic** after the disc's languages. It translates the European disc's English texts
  and is offered on a disc that carries them (not on the North American disc); the speech is any language your disc
  speaks.

- **A language pack is not a mod:** the Arabic text is one entry of the TEXT LANGUAGE row, like French or German; it has no
  switch anywhere and never moves your saves to the modded memory card.

## PS Vita

- **A PS Vita package in every release** (`twinsanityevolved-<version>-vita.vpk`): install it with VitaShell, copy your
  disc image to `ux0:data/twinsanity/game.iso`, and make sure the system's shader library `ur0:data/libshacccg.suprx` is
  present (the usual homebrew step). The package carries the game's GPU programs ready-made, so the first start does not
  stop to prepare them.
- **The launcher makes a ready-to-copy PS Vita folder** in your game folder (`vita/`: the package, the steps, and
  `ux0/data/twinsanity/` with your settings, the language pack and the mods you switched on). Two disc images can sit side
  by side on the console; `ux0:data/twinsanity/disc-image.txt` names the one the game starts. The North American disc
  plays on the PS Vita too (at 60). Mods are added on a computer and copied over.
- **The PAL console's 50 Hz game loop by default:** the game ticks 50 times a second as the PAL game did. GAME RATE in
  Options > graphic options switches between `60` and `50 hz (pal)` from the next start.
- **FRAME RATE auto:** the game runs at the full rate while it keeps up and otherwise holds a steady 25 (at 50 Hz) or 30
  (at 60 Hz) frames a second, so the picture moves evenly instead of swinging; `60` / `30` (`50` / `25`) fix the rate.
- **Fixed on the PS Vita:** the movies play (the boot logos and the story movies did not play at all); the intro movie
  of a new game is no longer corrupted; shadows are drawn; the title sequence's iceberg and logo draw cleanly; Cavern
  Catastrophe's puzzle crystals are visible; at 50 Hz the game no longer runs in slow motion with the sound running ahead
  of it; the GAME RATE setting takes effect.
- **Faster:** the drawing, the GPU work per picture, the geometry and the game code itself were all made quicker,
  measured scene by scene.
- **Still limited:** the heaviest levels and fights fall back to a steady 25 (at 50 Hz) or 30 (at 60 Hz; 20 in the very
  heaviest scenes) frames a second; importing mods is done on a computer.

## The launcher keeps itself up to date

- The launcher checks the releases when it starts (and from About): a newer launcher is offered on the Games page with one
  button; it downloads it, checks it against the release's SHA256SUMS, swaps itself and reopens. Your games, settings, saves
  and mods are not touched. The game's own **Update** button is unchanged. On the PS Vita the Installed list's **Update**
  fetches the new package into the PS Vita folder for you to install with VitaShell.
- The Mods page explains the PS Vita: switch mods here, press **Make the PS Vita folder**, copy `ux0/data/twinsanity` to the
  card. The folder's README names the disc file to copy and how two discs (European and North American) live side by side.

## PS Vita: faster in the heavy levels

- The refractive ice's frame copies (Ice Climb) are taken only when needed and only for the region drawn since the last
  one, with the same pictures: on the emulator the copy passes' pixels per picture fell from 6.1 M to 0.27 M.
- **A second PS Vita package to try:** `twinsanityevolved-<version>-vita-native.vpk` runs the game's code natively on the
  console's ARM processor instead of through a translation layer. In the emulator it is identical in game state, sound and
  picture sequence to the main package on every tested route, and its hot functions have about half the instructions; how
  much faster the console runs it is what the console's own log tells. Same title: installing it replaces the program and
  keeps `ux0:data/twinsanity/`; reinstall the main package to go back. The main package stays the proven one until the
  console's figures make the native one the default.

## Fixes

- Depth: surfaces drawn on top of other surfaces -- the light patterns on cave walls, the ripple rings around floating ice,
  decals -- no longer flicker or vanish in pieces as the camera moves. The game's depth values are kept exactly (the PS2's
  24-bit depth) instead of being rounded, so the depth test gives the PS2's answer everywhere (every system).
- PS Vita: entering a new area or loading a level no longer stalls the picture for whole frames: a chunk's textures are
  decoded and its meshes prepared in the background as the chunk arrives, instead of in the first frame that shows it.
- At 50 Hz (`game rate: 50 hz (pal)`), on every system, the game no longer plays in slow motion: the game and its sound
  run at real speed at 50 and at 60.
- A settings file holding the game folder's own keys (`paths.memcards`, `paths.mods`, `mods.order`) is read instead of
  being refused whole.
- A disc this version cannot play is refused at the start with a sentence saying why, instead of failing on a missing
  file.
- An update by the launcher keeps an older game folder's language setting (`game.language`).
